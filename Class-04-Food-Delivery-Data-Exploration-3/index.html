Before we start: read the shape

What is a.shape?
a = np.array([[1, 2, 3],
              [4, 5, 6]])
(2, 3). The outer bracket holds 2 lists: 2 rows. Each list holds 3 numbers: 3 columns. Rows first. Today every operation starts by reading the shapes.
Rows go down. Columns go across. The shape says rows first.

Before we start: arange, reshape and axis

What does this print?
a = np.arange(1, 7).reshape(2, 3)
print(a.sum(axis=0))
[5 7 9]. Step by step:
  1. np.arange(1, 7) makes [1 2 3 4 5 6]. The end, 7, is left out.
  2. reshape(2, 3) fills 2 rows of 3, in reading order: [[1 2 3] [4 5 6]].
  3. axis=0 adds down each column, so the rows disappear: 1 + 4 = 5, 2 + 5 = 7, 3 + 6 = 9.
With axis=1 you would get one total per row, [ 6 15]; with no axis, one number, 21.
axis=0 adds down each column: 5, 7, 9.
And this one?
b = np.arange(2, 14, 2)
b = b.reshape(3, -1)
print(b.sum(axis=1))
[ 6 14 22]. Step by step:
  1. np.arange(2, 14, 2) starts at 2 and jumps by 2, stopping before 14: [ 2 4 6 8 10 12], 6 numbers.
  2. reshape(3, -1): 3 rows, and -1 means "work out the columns". 6 numbers ÷ 3 rows = 2 columns: [[ 2 4] [ 6 8] [10 12]].
  3. axis=1 adds across each row, so the columns disappear: 2 + 4 = 6, 6 + 8 = 14, 10 + 12 = 22.
[18 24] is axis=0 (down each column); 42 is the sum with no axis.
axis=1 adds across each row: 6, 14, 22.

No: the columns were sorted apart

Broadcasting: what it is, and why

  • Broadcasting is how NumPy does maths on two arrays of different shapes: it stretches the smaller one to fit the bigger one.
  • Why: one fee, tax or discount can go onto every price in one line, with no loop and no copies of the data.
💼
Pricing: three dishes cost ₹100, ₹200 and ₹300. Add a ₹20 packing fee to each.
✋
An array of 3 and one number. What prints?
prices = np.array([100, 200, 300])
print(prices + 20)
[120 220 320]
[120 200 300]
An error: 3 numbers and 1 number
[120 220 320]
  • The 20 is stretched to [20 20 20], then added position by position.
  • Stretched, not copied: NumPy reuses the one 20. That is broadcasting, in its simplest form.
One number stretches to the length of the array. Dashed: reused, not stored.
Why the business needs this: one line adds the fee to 3 prices or to 3 lakh.

Two arrays: when it works, when it cannot

💼
Pricing: a different packing fee for each dish: ₹10, ₹20, ₹30. And then someone sends only two fees.
✋

prices = np.array([100, 200, 300])
print(prices + np.array([10, 20, 30]))
print(prices + np.array([10, 20]))
Both work
The first works; the second is an error
Both are errors
[110 220 330] ValueError: operands could not be broadcast together with shapes (3,) (2,)
  • First line: same length, 3 and 3. No stretching needed: position by position.
  • Second line: 3 prices and 2 fees. The third price has no fee, and 2 is not 1, so there is nothing NumPy can stretch.
Same length: each price meets its own fee.
Why it cannot work: the third price has no partner. Only a length of 1 can stretch; a length of 2 cannot.
Second arrayLengthWorks?

Why the business needs this: a fee list with a missing dish is caught at once, before a customer sees a wrong price.

Block recap: Broadcasting stretches the smaller array when the shapes fit from the right: equal, or 1. A 1 in the rows place stretches down, in the columns place right. Nothing is copied. Anything else is an error.

Recap

✍️
Two minutes. Three points you would tell a friend who missed today.
  • *, +, -, / work element by element. An array and a number: the number goes to every element.
  • @ (or np.matmul, np.dot) multiplies a row by a column and adds. (A, B) @ (B, C) gives (A, C).
  • Broadcasting stretches a smaller array when the shapes fit from the right: equal, or 1. Nothing is copied.
  • Carried over: np.sort for values, np.argsort for positions; sort a table by whole rows.
